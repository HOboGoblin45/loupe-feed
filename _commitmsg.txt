Classify accessory subtypes; drop trinkets from partner shelves

"accessories" was one bucket holding two different things: pieces someone opens
a fashion app to find (a bag, a hat, sunglasses) and trinkets that fill a swipe
deck without being what anyone came for (stud earrings, hair claws, tights, a
bookmark). Nothing downstream could tell them apart.

Every accessory and shoe now carries accessorySubtype, computed once here rather
than by regex on-device. Two things use it:

  * PARTNER SHELVES drop trinkets outright. A partner's presence in Loupe is a
    curated shelf we assemble on their behalf, not a mirror of their store, and
    Gemini's 400 pieces were 42% accessories -- 107 of them jewellery, hair
    claws, tights and scarves. Their shelf is now 293 pieces. Bags, hats and
    shoes stay: they read as fashion and, measured on Gemini's own numbers,
    earn more saves per piece than the jewellery did.

  * THE APP demotes them in Discover, remotely tunable, and never removes them
    (loupe-app, wTrinket). Trinkets are 14% of the whole catalog; deleting them
    would drop ~30 labels out of the app and the public brand directory over a
    ranking problem.

Classifier notes, all drawn from live titles:

  * _word_in gives word boundaries AND tolerates a trailing s/es, which matters
    more here than anywhere else: a naive \bstud\b misses "Croissant Studs".
  * Bare "ring" is guarded -- jewellery, unless it is "key ring", "O-ring strap"
    or "ring handle", which are hardware on something else.
  * Bare "claw" and "clip" count as hair. A clip-on EARRING landing there is
    harmless: both are trinkets, so the outcome is identical either way.
  * A GARMENT NOUN VETOES a trinket reading. Seven "Scarf Top" products were
    live -- real tops their store had filed under accessories -- and demoting
    them would have hidden clothing to solve a trinket problem. Those fall
    through to "other", which is kept and never demoted.
  * homeware is matched before jewellery so "Key Ring Charm Holder" reads as a
    keychain instead of being claimed by "charm".

test_accessory_subtype.py: 51 fixtures, wired into the CI gate alongside the
other three. Every case is a real title from the live catalog.
